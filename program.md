# Training Program — max hypertrophy per unit time

Derived 2026-07-27 from a four-lane evidence sweep (mechanisms / minimum-effective-dose / practitioner convergence / disconfirmation). Every prescription below traces to a dose-response finding, not a convention. Where lanes disagreed, the RCT/meta-analysis wins over expert consensus — noted inline.

## The five variables that actually move hypertrophy

Ranked by effect size per unit of time spent. Everything not on this list is noise.

1. **Weekly hard sets per muscle** — the only true dose variable. Curve: ~4 sets/wk is the floor for any growth (Iversen 2021), ~10 captures the large majority of achievable gains (Schoenfeld 2017 meta-regression), 10–20 is a broad responsive plateau (Baz-Valle 2022), returns flatten hard past ~20 (Pelland 2024/25 Bayesian meta-regression, 67 studies / 2058 subjects — full data and analysis code are open at https://osf.io/6z3xu, so this curve is inspectable rather than taken on trust). **Target 10–14.** Going 4→10 buys most of the growth; 10→20 buys a sliver for double the time.
2. **Proximity to failure** — non-linear and saturating. Fitted curve (Refalo 2023 meta, 15 studies): 5 RIR ≈ 75% of max stimulus, ~+5% per rep closer, so **1–2 RIR ≈ 90–95%**. A 2024 RCT (Refalo, J Sports Sci) found failure vs 1–2 RIR produced equal quadriceps growth. Failure costs disproportionate fatigue and joint stress for ~nothing.
3. **Progressive overload** — the thing sets and RIR are a delivery mechanism for. Requires stable exercise selection to be measurable at all.
4. **Full ROM with a loaded stretch** — stretch-mediated hypertrophy is real but small (d≈0.20, 2024 meta, 42 studies / 1318 subjects). Free: costs zero extra time, so take it.
5. **Rest long enough to preserve load** — >60s beats <60s (Singer 2024 Bayesian meta), negligible benefit past ~90s. Short rest only "works" by degrading the next set's volume load.

## What has NO independent effect (stop optimizing it)

- **Rep range / load, 30–85% 1RM** — trivial pooled ES 0.03 (95% CI −0.16 to 0.22) when sets are matched and near failure. Pick loads by joint comfort and fatigue economy, not by a magic rep number. Load still matters for *strength* (specificity), just not size.
- **Frequency at matched volume** — 1x vs 2x vs 3x/wk per muscle: no difference (Schoenfeld 2019, 25 studies; reaffirmed Pelland 2025). Frequency is a volume-*distribution* tool. Its only real benefit is indirect: spreading sets prevents junk volume late in a session.
- **Split choice** — full-body vs upper/lower vs bro-split: no difference at equated volume (2024 meta, 14 studies). Choose by time budget. Classic 5-day bro splits are strictly worse *logistically* (more gym visits, 1x frequency) for zero growth benefit.
- **Tempo** — trivial (SMD ≈ 0.09) anywhere from 0.5–8 s/rep. Prescribing "3-1-2-0" is wasted attention.
- **Instability work** — lowers force output, therefore lowers mechanical tension. Zero hypertrophy case for a healthy lifter.
- **Pre-workout feeding** — 12-wk RCT (2025, IJSNEM) found fasted vs fed produced equal hypertrophy at matched daily protein. Train whenever is convenient.
- **General warm-ups** — cardio and unrelated mobility add nothing. 2–4 ramping sets of the first working lift covers it.

## Time-compression: the single highest-ROI lever

These hold the stimulus constant while cutting session length. They are substitutions, not additions.

- **Antagonist supersets** (press/row, curl/pushdown): −36–50% session time, no chronic hypertrophy penalty (2024/25 meta). The whole program is built on these.
- **Drop sets**: −30–70% per-exercise time, trivial difference vs straight sets across three independent metas (Coleman 2022 ES 0.08, Sødal 2023 ES 0.155, 2026 Sports Med-Open).
- **Myo-reps / rest-pause**: match traditional sets on hypertrophy, shorter, better perceived recovery.
- **Rest discipline**: 90–120 s compounds, 60–90 s isolation. The 2–3 min default is padding.

## The program — four fixed sessions, 4× / week, ~32–38 min

**Still only 13 distinct exercises.** Restructured 2026-07-31: the old two full-body templates (alternated 3×/week, ~55–70 min each) become four fixed, individually-themed sessions (4×/week, ~32–38 min each) carrying the *identical* weekly set total, just redistributed across more, shorter visits.

**Why 3→4 costs zero growth:** frequency at matched volume has no measured effect (above: 1x vs 2x vs 3x/wk per muscle — no difference, Schoenfeld 2019, 25 studies; reaffirmed Pelland 2025), and split choice has no measured effect at equated volume (above: full-body vs upper/lower vs bro-split — no difference, 2024 meta, 14 studies). Redistributing the same weekly sets across a 4th session is a direct application of both findings already in this document, not a new bet. What it buys: each session now carries roughly 3/4 the exercise load of the old templates (shorter, not harder), and every isolation lift now sits in exactly 2 of the 4 sessions instead of every session. Under the old A-B-A/B-A-B alternation, standing calf raise (A5+B4b) and lateral raise (A3b+B4a) landed in *every single workout* — a lazy fix for the calf-volume gap that also made two isolation lifts the least varied part of the entire program. The new structure fixes that by construction, not by patching (exercises.md, restructure rationale).

Each of the 13 exercises now has exactly one fixed weekly slot-count instead of the old ~1.5×/week fractional alternation: most compounds run 1×/week, all 5 isolation lifts run exactly 2×/week, same total volume as before (arithmetic below). Still one progression line per exercise — a cleaner one, since "sets this week" is now a fixed number instead of depending on which week of the A-B-A/B-A-B cycle it was.

Sub-letters (1a/1b) are supersets — alternate back and forth, busy-gym rule below. Days 3 and 4 run as straight sets deliberately (no antagonist pairing available, and Day 4 is already the fast, all-machine day).

That's still the **low shoulder of the responsive plateau, deliberately.** It's where marginal growth per marginal minute is highest. If you later want more, add a set to 1-1a/2-1b/3-1/4-1 rather than new exercises.

Stretch-bias is taken wherever it's free: overhead extension over pushdowns, seated leg curl over lying (hip flexed = longer hamstring), deep leg press, RDL. (The former "incline curl over preacher" stretch claim was reclassified as unconfirmed once the DB torque-curve confound was controlled for — standing DB curl is now primary on friction grounds, incline is a fixed sub, not a stretch-bias pick; see exercises.md.)

**Rectus femoris tradeoff, acknowledged:** hack squat, leg press, and Bulgarian split squat are all simultaneous hip+knee-extension patterns, and RF must minimize force output during combined hip+knee extension to avoid opposing hip extension (Ema 2016) — so this selection biases quad growth toward the vasti. A 12-wk within-subject MRI RCT comparing leg press directly to knee extension found RF grew +13.2% (p≤.001) with knee extension vs +1.1% (p=.379, not significant) with leg press, despite comparable whole-quadriceps growth between conditions (Kinoshita/Maeo 2026, MSSE). RF is ~10% of quad cross-sectional volume, so this is accepted deliberately for growth-per-minute, not an oversight. Swap one Bulgarian-split-squat slot for leg extension later if central/proximal quad appearance becomes a priority.

### Monotony measures (2026-07-31)

Shorter sessions fix length; they don't automatically fix boring. Four changes target engagement specifically, at zero volume/RIR/progression cost:
- **No isolation lift appears in more than 2 of 4 sessions** (table above) — the actual fix for the old every-session calf-raise/lateral-raise defect. Every isolation lift's exposure is stated and capped at 2-of-4; none is in 3 or all 4.
- **Free-choice slots** on lateral raise (DB or cable) and curl (standing or incline) — both pairs are RCT-proven growth-equivalent (exercises.md, A3b/B4a and A4b), so picking whichever is available or feels better that day costs nothing.
- **Set-structure variety**: myo-reps on calf raise (both weekly instances), drop-sets on triceps extension (both instances), and a "bulk of the sets at RIR 1, one finisher set at RIR 0" pattern on every other isolation lift — all cited above as hypertrophy-equivalent to straight sets (lines 30–31). Each exercise uses the *same* structure on both of its weekly appearances — only the set count differs, same precedent as the old program's asymmetric A5/B4b and A3b/B4a set counts — so the progression log stays one clean line per exercise, not two different metrics to reconcile.
- **RIR-0 exposure reduced, not increased.** Only one set per isolation exercise per session is pushed to true RIR 0 (the drop-set or finisher); the rest sit at RIR 1. The old program ran several isolation blocks at RIR 0 for every set.

### Fixed substitutions (decide once, never again)

Busy gym means occupied stations, not new exercises. One pre-approved swap per lift. These subs are pre-vetted as growth-equivalent (exercises.md), so use them liberally — not only when the station is physically taken, but whenever setup cost or skill demand is what's standing between you and the session happening at all. If the sub is also taken, do the other lift in the superset pair as straight sets and come back.

**Anti-rationalization line:** that permission covers logistics — setup, wait, coordination — never the working sets themselves. RDL and Bulgarian split squat stay as prescribed even on a day you don't feel like them: the discomfort of loading a long muscle length *is* the stimulus there, not a separable cost (exercises.md, anti-rationalization test).

### Busy-gym superset rule

Pairs are chosen so both halves usually live near each other: 1-1a/1-1b are DB rack + row machine, 1-3a/1-3b are now both at the DB rack (one station, two DB pairs — no holding, no etiquette question), 2-1a/2-1b are bench/dip station + pulldown, 2-2a/2-2b are DB rack (± cable stack). Days 3 and 4 are straight sets by design — no antagonist pairing exists for RDL/BSS, and Day 4 is already the short, all-machine day. Rules of engagement:
1. Only hold two stations if the gym is quiet enough that it isn't rude.
2. If you can't, run straight sets at **60–90 s rest** and accept ~10–15 extra minutes on Day 1 or 2. The superset saves time; it does not add growth. Nothing is lost but the clock.
3. Never let station-hunting break the rep log. A substituted lift gets logged as the substitute, on its own progression line.

### Adherence — the third growth variable

Per-session stimulus × completion rate × weeks sustained (exercises.md, "What optimal means"). These three moves buy completion rate and weeks-sustained at ~zero stimulus cost — cheap insurance, not new stimulus, so they don't show up in the set/rep tables above.

- **Minimum viable session.** Short on time or motivation: run only the first pair/exercise of that day (1-1a/1-1b, 2-1a/2-1b, 3-1, or 4-1) at 2 sets each, same RIR, and stop. Exception-only floor for a bad day, not a program variant — treating it as the default forfeits most of the week's volume. Log it as a partial session, not a skip.
- **Time ceiling.** Session running past ~45 min: drop the last isolation slot (1-3b, 2-3, 3-4, or 4-4) rather than rushing the working sets or grinding through. Costs the lowest-priority volume in the program — side/rear delts, arms, calves are already at the deliberately low shoulder of the responsive plateau — never the compounds. **Drop the named slot, not whichever station is occupied.** On 2026-08-12 the slot that vanished was 1-3a, rear delts — the *least* well-dosed muscle in the program at 5 sets/wk — purely because the pec deck was busy. Station availability is not a priority ranking; it selects the wrong victim. 1-3b (side delts, 8 sets/wk) is the designated one.
- **Fixed schedule.** Same four days/times each week, not a floating target. Context-consistency is the most robustly replicated habit-formation lever (medium confidence, general-behavior evidence, not RT-specific) and it costs zero stimulus.

## Progression

**Double progression** — RCT-supported (2023, PMID 36515591: rep-zone progression matched or beat percentage-based on CSA; 2024 systematic review found no superiority for load-only). Rules:
1. Hit the top of the rep range for *all* sets at the prescribed RIR.
2. Next session, add the smallest available load increment and drop back to the bottom of the range.
3. Log every set. Untracked training cannot progressively overload — this is what makes exercise-churn ("muscle confusion") actively harmful. Keep the same lifts 6–12+ weeks.

## Return-to-training ramp — 1 week, with one exception

**The scan overrides the self-report.** Stated history is "about a year off, wasn't super into it." But the InBody history shows SMM 75.2 → 76.5 lb across those 14 months. Muscle mass went *up*. Detrained people lose skeletal muscle; he didn't. So this is not a layoff — it's a long stretch of low-intensity, inconsistent training that successfully maintained tissue (entirely consistent with the maintenance literature: 1–3 near-failure sets/muscle/week held size over 32 weeks).

That invalidates the standard ramp math. The "2× the layoff" rule would prescribe two years of ramping, which is absurd, because the rule assumes genuine detraining and the body-composition data says otherwise.

What *is* still deconditioned is tolerance for **heavy load specifically**. "Wasn't super into it" means light and inconsistent, and tendon/connective-tissue stiffness adapts to load magnitude, more slowly than muscle does — the asynchrony that makes fast-ramping returners the ones who get hurt. Muscle mass being intact says nothing about tendon readiness for a heavy RDL.

**Cut from 3 weeks to 1 on 2026-07-31, deliberately.** The reasoning above survives; the *duration* did not. Nothing measured "3 weeks" — no trial randomizes ramp length in returning lifters and reports injury rates. It was a convention, and this document's entire method is refusing convention where nothing measured it (see the deload section, which overrides expert consensus on exactly this basis). The mechanism — tendon stiffness lagging muscle — supports *a* ramp. It does not specify three weeks, and a 3× overshoot on a guess costs two weeks of training.

**Ramp (1 week):**
- **Week 1:** full program, all exercises, 2 sets each, RIR 4. Loads that feel almost too easy. This week is for movement pattern re-learning and finding working weights, not stimulus.
- **Week 2+:** program as written, double progression running.

**The one exception — Romanian deadlift, 3 weeks.** The ramp is not uniformly justified across 13 exercises, which is the flaw in a global ramp. A lateral raise and a seated leg curl carry near-zero connective-tissue risk; there is nothing to ramp. The RDL is different in kind: loaded lumbar flexion at a long muscle length, the highest-consequence failure mode in the program and the one lift where the injury isn't a missed session but a missed quarter. So RDL alone keeps the original schedule — RIR 4 week 1, RIR 3 week 2, RIR 2 week 3, working weight from week 4. Everything else goes to prescribed loads from week 2.

That is the substantive change: the ramp moves from a property of the *calendar* to a property of the *lift*, which is more precise than either 1 or 3 weeks flat.

Expect the reps to feel available immediately — that feeling is not evidence, and it is the reason the RDL exception exists. The muscle is ready; the tendons are the constraint; there is no proprioceptive signal that tells you which one you're testing.

**Canary, since the safety margin is now thinner:** new joint or tendon pain that persists >48 h, or a sharp change in a lift's rep quality at unchanged load, means back off that lift by ~15% and re-ramp it over two weeks. This is a per-lift rule, not a program-wide reset.

Baseline injury risk is genuinely low: bodybuilding-style RT runs 0.24–1 injuries per 1000 training hours, among the safest modalities and several times safer than running. The lever is load-ramp discipline, *not* avoiding compounds or training cautiously forever.

## Deloads — autoregulated, not scheduled

Lane conflict, resolved toward the harder evidence. Coach consensus (Delphi 2023; 2024 survey: every 5.6 ± 2.3 wks) says deload on a calendar. But a 2024 study inserting a 1-wk deload mid-program found it *hurt* lower-body strength with no hypertrophy benefit, and longitudinal RCT support for periodic unloading is essentially absent. Expert convergence isn't evidence.

**Rule:** no scheduled deloads. Take one when performance actually regresses — reps drop at the same load across two sessions, or joints/sleep degrade. Then run 1 week at ~half volume, same exercises, +3 RIR. Maintenance is dirt cheap (1–3 near-failure sets/muscle/week held size over 32 weeks in volume-reduction work), so a light week costs nothing. The initial ramp phase serves as the first deload.

## Interaction with the nutrition side

Near-maintenance calories (see CLAUDE.md) means the growth rate ceiling is low regardless of programming — which *strengthens* the case for the 10–14 set band over 20+. Extra volume at maintenance buys fatigue, not tissue. If the weight trend goes flat for 3+ weeks with progression stalling, the fix is +150 kcal, not +5 sets.

## Resolved inputs

- **Training history:** ~1 year of low-intensity, inconsistent training (not a true layoff — SMM rose over the period). Ramp set to 1 week (RDL alone keeps 3) — see the ramp section for why the duration was cut and the exception kept.
- **Gym:** busy but well-equipped. Program constrained to 13 exercises with one fixed substitute each; supersets are optional time-savers, not requirements.

## What to expect

Because SMM was maintained rather than lost, there is **less "muscle memory" regain available than a true returner gets** — the fast, free window of re-growth largely isn't there. Progress will look like normal trained-lifter progress: slow. At near-maintenance calories, roughly 0.25–0.5 lb of lean mass per month is a realistic ceiling, and it will be invisible on a single InBody scan given the ±3–4 point PBF error.

Judge the program on **logbook progression over 8 weeks**, not on scan numbers. If loads and reps are climbing on the main lifts, it's working, whatever the scan says at the October retest.
