# Standing desk: is it useful? (2026-08-30, deep-research sweep)

Method: 97-agent, 3-round research harness. 8 initial angles (measured energy cost, inactivity
mechanism, postprandial trials, MSK RCTs, cognition, dedicated null-results lens, "does training
cancel sitting risk", cheaper substitutes) plus 5 follow-up angles chosen by a critic (harms of
standing, lean-subject trials, causal-inference designs, ≥12-month RCTs, provenance of the
recommendation). 53 sources fetched, 168 claims extracted, 6 contested and excluded.
Priced on `cardio.md`'s four axes in order: energy, adaptation, interference, risk.

## Verdict

**Don't buy for health. Buy only as furniture you prefer, with the health claim dropped.**
The one thing the literature shows works is *transitions* (sit-to-stand, chair stands, 2-min
walks every 20–30 min). A desk sells sustained standing, which is the null exposure. A free timer
delivers the active ingredient.

## Axis 1: energy — retired

| Input | Value | Source tier |
|---|---|---|
| Standing minus sitting | 0.15 kcal/min pooled, 0.19 in men (46 studies, n=1184, I²=92%) | meta-analysis |
| Achieved standing dose in RCTs | 28–83 min/day, decays to ~57 min/day at 3–12 months | RCT / Cochrane |
| Honest daily delta | **~6–13 kcal/day** | arithmetic, not published |
| Famous "54 kcal/day, 2.5 kg/yr" | assumes 6 h/day standing, never achieved in any trial | debunked |
| vs the ±150 kcal step | 4–8% of one step | — |

Internal consistency check: treadmill desks add ~105 kcal/h (12× standing) and still moved no
glucose, lipid, BP or body-fat endpoint across 13 studies. If 105 kcal/h does nothing measurable,
9 kcal/h cannot. Energy is not the active ingredient anywhere in this literature.

Two inversions worth keeping. Any sub-threshold offset is absorbed by the weekly weigh-in loop
rather than accumulating, so the "it adds up over a year" argument is only valid for people who
never weigh themselves. And in a surplus, 10 lost kcal is a (trivial) cost, not a benefit.

## Axis 2: adaptation — already saturated

- Harmonised meta-analysis, >1M people: sitting >8 h/day at ~60–75 min/day of moderate activity
  gave HR 1.04 (0.99–1.10), i.e. no excess risk. Accelerometer pooling (n=44,370) puts the
  threshold lower, at 30–40 min/day MVPA. Four lifts plus squash clears that several times over.
- The converse is the useful finding: people sitting <4 h/day but in the lowest activity quartile
  still had HR 1.27. Activity volume is the variable, not posture.
- The sedentary signal is domain-specific and lives in TV time (MR: +1.5 h/day TV → CAD OR 1.44),
  not computer/occupational sitting. In 4,811 civil servants over 13 years, neither total, work,
  nor non-TV home sitting predicted diabetes.
- The underlying sitting-mortality estimates are inflated 13–26% by reverse causation; with a
  5-year exclusion the top-vs-bottom sedentary HR was 1.31 (0.80–2.17), non-significant.

## Postprandial glucose/insulin — the conflation at the heart of the sales pitch

| Exposure | Contractile content | Result in crossover trials |
|---|---|---|
| Sitting | none | referent |
| Prolonged standing (= the desk) | tonic isometric | null on insulin, null on glucose, blood flow bump gone by 60 min, FMD unchanged |
| Standing *breaks* (5 min / 30 min) | ~16 transitions/day | +34% glucose / 20% insulin in 66-y.o. dysglycemic women; non-significant in office workers |
| 2-min walks or 1-min chair stands | phasic large-muscle work | glucose −24 to −55%, insulin −28 to −37%, pooled SMD −0.30 |

Chair stands (1 min every 30 min) *beat* 2-min treadmill walks on post-lunch insulin (−37% vs
−29%). The dose variable is contraction events, not posture and not kcal. Muscle LPL suppression
is an inactivity-specific signal that light ambulatory contraction prevents. Quiet standing is
closer to sitting than to walking on that axis, so the standing nulls are what the mechanism
predicts, not a failure of it. Two independent author teams concluded the message should be
"move often", not "stand instead of sit".

Every positive result comes from a population with headroom this user lacks: older, overweight,
dysglycemic or inactive subjects. No trial enrolled a lean trained lifter. In a healthy subject
glucose is defended and the cost shows up in insulin, so glucose iAUC is the wrong endpoint
anyway. Benefit is unestablished here, not proven absent.

Why the chronic desk RCTs are null: not because the acute effect fails to accumulate, but because
(a) standing's acute effect is absent in healthy people to begin with, and (b) at 6–13 kcal/day
and n=19–127, the chronic trials could never have detected it. Nulls here are non-measurements.

## Axis 3: interference — none

Cognitively and productively neutral. 44-person crossover: cognition unchanged or improved, typing
errors unchanged, typing speed down ~7%. SMaT at 12 months: no cognitive difference, self-reported
engagement up, sickness absence unchanged. No plausible effect on lifting at any realistic dose.

## MSK / posture

Larger trials (SMaT, n=127, 12 months; 24-month RCT) show no between-group MSK benefit and full
fade-out. Positive results are within-group or n≈30. Safe for a healthy back, but there is no
symptom here to relieve, and `posture.md` already settled that slouch is a motor default a cue
fixes, not a tissue problem furniture fixes. One 6-month cluster RCT of a dynamic seat cushion
reported neck/LBP incidence HR 0.16–0.19 — implausibly large, unblinded, unreplicated, high-risk
workers. Do not act on it.

## Axis 4: risk — non-zero, and it rises with the same variable as the benefit

- Accelerometer cohort (preprint): standing >2 h/day gave ~11% higher orthostatic circulatory
  disease risk per extra 30 min, with no CVD benefit from standing time. Sitting >10 h/day also
  raised CVD ~15%/h, so this is not a case for sitting either.
- Occupational standing: fatigue, leg swelling, varicose veins in ~36–50% of all-day standers.
  Cannot separate intermittent desk standing from continuous occupational standing.
- Cost-effectiveness: the desk-plus-programme arm was dominated (more cost, fewer QALYs) by the
  identical behavioural programme without the desk.

## Behaviour: what a desk actually changes

Sitting reduction of 84–116 min/day short term decays to ~57 min/day, and one 24-month RCT faded
to null. Leisure sitting rose ~23 min/day in intervention arms (compensation, non-significant).
Both large UK trials report sitting was converted into standing with no MVPA change, which is the
one conversion the mechanism says does not matter. The paradox: furniture has the best delivery
and the null mechanism, movement breaks have the best mechanism and the worst delivery (Cochrane:
walk-break prompts had no effect on total sitting time).

## Evidence quality

Cochrane rates sit-stand evidence on sitting time low to very low (34 studies, n=3,397). The
energy meta-analysis has I²=92%. A lead author of the widely cited "stand 2–4 h/day" guidelines
owns 100% of a sit-stand products company and runs the "Get Britain Standing" campaign, a
conflict omitted from most coverage. 30–40% of media stories on sedentary behaviour carry the
"sitting undoes exercise" message, which the harmonised data contradict directly.

Nothing in this literature (insulin iAUC, LPL, FMD, IMT) is measurable with a scale and a
Liftosaur log. Even if a desk worked, this project could never see it.

## What to do instead (free)

1. 25–30 min timer. On each fire: 1 min of body-weight chair stands with a calf raise, or a 2-min
   walk. That is the exact exposure the positive trials tested.
2. Wear the cue habit from `posture.md`; it is the same class of intervention.
3. Nothing else. Adherence to the lifting program is worth ~15 points of hypertrophy; this whole
   question is worth ~0.

## Reopens if

- A trial randomises sit-to-stand transition count as the exposure and shows a desk raises it.
- Lifting adherence collapses back to the 1.6 sessions/week failure mode, removing the MVPA
  offset. Even then the desk is the wrong purchase, because it delivers sustained standing rather
  than transitions.
- Back pain appears. Then the question is symptom relief, and a 29-person LBP RCT is the relevant
  (weak) evidence.

## Open questions the sweep could not close

- Does a sit-stand desk increase daily transition count, or just swap one static posture for
  another? Unmeasured anywhere.
- What is the postprandial insulin response in a lean trained adult, and is there any iAUC left
  to reduce?
- Does the orthostatic risk apply to intermittent desk standing or only continuous occupational
  standing?
- A two-week CGM would be the only cheap way to test a break protocol on this n=1 rather than
  argue from other populations. Not worth doing given the effect-size ranking above.
