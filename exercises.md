# Exercise selection — rationale and adversarial audit

Derived 2026-07-31 from a five-lane adversarial pass (framework, lower body, push, pull, structure) plus skeptic verification against 2022–2026 longitudinal RCTs and meta-analyses. EMG amplitude is excluded as evidence throughout — activation is not growth. Where an EMG-only argument was the best case for an alternative, the alternative lost.

## What optimal means

Revised 2026-07-31. **Expected growth ≈ per-session stimulus × completion rate × weeks sustained.** The four 2026-07-27 criteria optimized the first term only, silently assuming the other two were 1.0 — every prescribed session happens, forever. Neither is free for someone rebuilding a training habit in a busy commercial gym.

### What stays off-limits

Three variables carry the program's entire measured hypertrophy effect (program.md's ranked list): **weekly hard sets per muscle, proximity to failure (RIR), progressive overload.** Adherence-weighting never touches them. Trimming a set, adding RIR as a comfort concession, or breaking the progression log is a stimulus cut wearing a friction costume — it debits the one term extra weeks-sustained can't buy back 1:1.

### Why friction is nearly free to spend

Program.md's evidence sweep already found that at equated volume and matched effort, split choice, frequency distribution, and — the load-bearing fact here — individual exercise choice mostly don't move growth (cable vs DB lateral raise is RCT-equivalent; incline vs standing curl shows no proven length-position advantage once the DB torque confound is controlled; rep range/load is noise). *Which exercise fills a slot* is therefore one of the only genuinely free variables left: changing it costs ~0 stimulus as long as volume, RIR, and overload hold. Friction is the cheapest currency for buying completion rate and weeks-sustained — spending it doesn't touch the account that has a hypertrophy balance.

### The break-even arithmetic

Illustrative, not measured. Over a 24-week block at 3×/week (~72 scheduled sessions):

- 10%-better exercise, completed 70% of the time: 72 × 0.70 × 1.10 ≈ **55.4** stimulus-sessions.
- No stimulus edge, completed 95% of the time: 72 × 0.95 × 1.00 ≈ **68.4**.

The "worse" exercise wins by ~24%; the better one breaks even only at ≥86.4% completion, a bar most people don't clear on the lift they dread. The asymmetry is structural: a per-exercise stimulus edge dilutes across ~1/13th of a session's volume, while dread or complexity removes 100% of that slot's stimulus — or, if the session gets skipped, 100% of everything. Separately, median tracked-app adherence in a large free-living cohort runs ~14 weeks before dropout versus a completed 24-week block: a ~1.7× swing in stimulus-weeks from adherence alone.

### Confidence, graded honestly

The hypertrophy RCTs cited below (Maeo, Chaves, Kinoshita) are dose-response outcome trials on the exact comparison being made. The adherence evidence is not that kind of evidence and shouldn't be read with the same confidence: no RCT randomizes exercise selection or friction and measures dropout. What exists — (a) between-study duration/frequency comparisons, confounded rather than causal; (b) supervised-trial adherence (81–92%) running well above free-living adherence (18–68%, population- and timeframe-dependent), which is a reason to distrust supervised-RCT adherence numbers for a free-living gym-goer; (c) autonomy/self-selection RCTs showing better in-session affect at matched effort, with affect-predicts-later-participation inferred from the general physical-activity literature, not proven in a resistance-training outcome trial; (d) a Delphi expert-consensus instrument rating exercise technical complexity as a plausible barrier, with no dropout numbers attached. Every claim in this section is **low-to-medium confidence**. The mechanism and the arithmetic carry the argument, not any single cited percentage.

### The five criteria, reordered

1. **Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.** Still first. An exercise that can't reach 1–2 RIR on the target muscle isn't a candidate, however convenient. Removing a balance or bracing demand removes a competing limiter, so the target muscle can reach failure territory.
2. **Adherence / friction — new, ranked here deliberately, not as a footnote.** Setup cost, station contention, skill/coordination demand, decision load when time pressure peaks. Above measurability and stretch because the arithmetic shows the completion-rate gap between a convenient and an inconvenient exercise dwarfs the stimulus gap between two exercises hitting the same muscle at matched volume/RIR. Logistics and cognitive load, not discomfort — see the test below.
3. **Progression measurability.** Small, clean load or rep increments session over session, with the *target muscle* as the limiter — not grip, not balance, not a coarse plate jump.
4. **Loaded stretch, where free.** Real but small (d≈0.20 pooled, program.md). Tiebreaker only, never chased at the expense of criteria 1–3.
5. **Resistance-curve match to strength curve.** Weakest in practice; overridden by outcome data whenever an RCT contradicts the mechanistic prediction (cable vs DB lateral raise, PMC12277279).

### The anti-rationalization test

Before dropping or swapping an exercise on friction grounds:

- **Committed-counterfactual:** would you still drop it if the friction were zero — equipment set up, nobody waiting, technique grooved? Yes → discomfort-avoidance, doesn't count. No, the objection is the setup, the wait, or the skill barrier → legitimate.
- **Sharper version:** a friction complaint is legitimate only if it targets something outside the five hypertrophy-driving variables (sets, RIR, overload, loaded stretch, rest). If it targets the near-failure sets or the loaded-stretch position itself, it's avoidance — for the RDL and Bulgarian split squat, the discomfort of a long muscle length under real load *is* the mechanism, not a separable cost. Both stay exactly as prescribed; see the Friction audit.

Excluded as decision criteria, unchanged: EMG amplitude (activation ≠ growth), novelty/variety (weak-to-mixed evidence, actively harms progression measurability), and "feel"/pump as a standalone signal.

## The 4×/week restructure (2026-07-31)

**Objective.** The constraint, stated twice: 3–4 gym visits/week is fine, but the two-template program (3×/week, ~55–70 min/session) was "not too complicated of exercises... something I'd do is better than something better that I wouldn't," and the sessions were "not max science but boring and long." He also caught a real defect: under A-B-A/B-A-B alternation, standing calf raise and lateral raise landed in *every single session*, because the calf-volume fix (finding 2) pasted a second calf slot into both templates instead of redesigning around it. Frequency was never the complaint; length and repetitiveness were.

**Why the 4th session is free.** Frequency at matched volume doesn't move hypertrophy (program.md line 18, Schoenfeld 2019/Pelland 2025); split choice doesn't move it at equated volume (program.md line 19, 2024 meta). Same weekly sets, one more container.

**What changed.** Two alternating full-body templates (~1.5×/week per exercise) became four fixed themed sessions: Day 1 press/pull I (chest, back, triceps, rear+side delts), Day 2 press/pull II (chest, back, biceps, side delts, calves), Day 3 hinge + unilateral (heaviest — RDL + BSS, straight sets), Day 4 squat + curl (fast, all-machine). Fixed weekly slot-counts: 8 of 13 compounds 1×/week, all 5 isolation lifts exactly 2×/week. The calf/lateral-raise defect falls out of the session themes instead of needing a patch.

**Monotony measures:**

- **2-of-4 cap per isolation lift** — every isolation exercise, not just the two that were caught.
- **Free-choice slots** on lateral raise (DB or cable) and curl (standing or incline) — both RCT-proven equivalent (1-3b/2-2b, 2-2a/3-4 below), so varying session-to-session costs zero stimulus and adds an autonomy lever where boredom is highest.
- **Set-structure variety** — myo-reps on calf raise, drop-sets on triceps extension, RIR-1-bulk / RIR-0-finisher on the rest. All hypertrophy-equivalent to straight sets (program.md lines 30–31).
- **RIR-0 exposure reduced** — one failure set per isolation exercise per session; the old program ran some isolation blocks at RIR 0 across every set. Consecutive RIR-0 isolation sets are plausibly their own dread source, independent of session length.

**Progression-integrity fix.** Three independent designs were drafted and cross-judged. The winner's source draft let an exercise use *different* set structures on its two weekly appearances — more varied, but it breaks the single-progression-line requirement, since "top of range at RIR 1" stops meaning the same thing session to session. Fixed: same technique on both instances, only set count differs — the precedent A5/B4b (3-vs-3) and A3b/B4a (3-vs-2) already set.

**A citation corrected.** The source draft claimed program.md "flags that RDL should never share a session with the quad-primary compound's heavy loading," as a problem this restructure solves. It isn't one — the two never shared a session under the old program either (RDL was Template B only). RDL and BSS *did* share old Template B and still share new Day 3 (3-1/3-2): retained on documented precedent, not introduced as a fix.

## Per-exercise rationale

### 4-1 — Hack squat or leg press (quad primary)
**Target:** vastus lateralis/medialis/intermedius primary; rectus femoris secondary (see tradeoff below).
**Why this beats back squat/Smith/pendulum:** the fixed torso removes the balance and spinal-bracing demand of a free-standing squat. No 2022–2026 RCT shows back squat beating leg press/hack squat for quad hypertrophy at matched effort, and machine support lets a deconditioned-tendon returner load quads near failure at far lower axial and systemic cost. Deep ROM collects the loaded stretch for free; plate/pin increments keep double progression clean.
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 2, 3.
**Sacrifices:** rectus femoris growth (see below) and transfer to free-squat skill, not a program goal.
**Gap noted, not acted on:** pendulum squat (upright torso, ascending resistance curve, low knee shear) is mechanistically at least as good and likely gentler on knees during the ramp, but wasn't on the fixed-substitute list. Cost-free as a second pre-approved sub; doesn't change the primary pick.

### 1-1a — Incline DB press (chest primary)
**Target:** clavicular (upper) pec primary; sternal/costal pec, anterior delt, triceps secondary.
**Why incline as primary, not flat:** Chaves 2020 (8-wk RCT, n=47, PMID 32922646) found incline-only training produced significantly greater upper-pec hypertrophy *and* statistically similar mid/lower-pec growth versus flat-only or combined. Incline-as-primary forfeits nothing; flat-as-primary leaves upper pec underserved.
**Wins on:** criterion 1; uniquely avoids a directional growth loss the reverse ordering would create.
**Sacrifices:** nothing measurable — a rare case of strict dominance.
**No dedicated overhead press:** anterior deltoid is trained only indirectly via 1-1a/2-1a. A 10-wk MRI RCT (flat bench only, 3x/wk, n=24) found anterior deltoid CSA grew 15% from horizontal pressing alone, against pec major's 20%. An OHP slot would displace one of only 2 pressing slots/session for a muscle already growing as a press byproduct.

### 1-1b — Chest-supported row (horizontal pull)
**Target:** lats, mid-traps, rhomboids; rear delts and biceps secondary.
**Why this beats free-standing/bent-over row:** stabilization demand lowers force output and therefore mechanical tension (program.md line 21). Chest support removes lower-back stabilization as the limiter, letting lats/mid-traps train closer to true failure. No 2022–2026 RCT heads chest-supported row against free row for regional back hypertrophy — a real evidence gap, so this rests on mechanism plus the program's verified instability principle, not row-specific outcome data.
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 2.
**Sacrifices:** the grip/stabilizer training a free row would add, not a program goal. With 2-1b (vertical pull, Day 2), both pulling vectors are covered.

### 4-2 — Seated leg curl (hamstring primary)
**Target:** biceps femoris long head, semitendinosus, semimembranosus.
**Why seated beats lying/prone:** Maeo et al. 2021 (MSSE 53(4):825-837, 12-wk within-subject RCT) found seated produced significantly greater hypertrophy in all three hamstring heads than prone (+14.1% vs +9.3%) at similar muscle-damage risk. Hip flexed at ~90° holds the biarticular hamstrings at a longer length throughout the set.
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 3 — one of the few picks backed by a direct hypertrophy-outcome RCT on the exact exercise-vs-exercise comparison.
**Sacrifices:** nothing identified. Lying leg curl is the fixed substitute, no caveat needed.

### 1-3b / 2-2b — DB lateral raise (side delt), free choice DB or cable
**Target:** lateral (middle) deltoid.
**Why DB over cable as primary (flipped 2026-07-31, friction criterion):** a 2025 RCT (Frontiers in Physiology, PMC12277279 / PMID 40692697, n=24, 8-wk, within-participant) found no meaningful difference between cable and DB lateral raises on lateral deltoid thickness — moderate-to-extreme statistical support for equivalence. That equivalence originally validated DB as the fixed substitute; it does the same work in reverse. Under the old templates this slot (then A3b) shared the cable stack with the triceps-extension slot (then A4a) in the same superset — the worst structural contention point audited here. DB needs a dumbbell rack instead, at zero measured stimulus cost. Cable's constant-tension curve is a real mechanical difference from DB's ascending-then-descending curve, but per criterion 5 it doesn't translate to growth — which is also why this became a free-choice slot.
**Wins on:** criterion 2 directly; criteria 1, 3, 4 empirically indistinguishable from cable; criterion 5 favors cable in theory only.
**Sacrifices:** nothing measured. Runs twice a week (1-3b + 2-2b, 4 sets each = 8/wk) rather than diversifying angle — but side delts land at only ~8 sets/wk, below the 10–14 target, so this isn't redundancy needing a trim.

### 1-2 / 3-3 — Overhead cable triceps extension
**Target:** triceps brachii, long-head emphasis.
**Why overhead beats pushdown/neutral position:** Maeo et al. 2023 (European Journal of Sport Science, PMID 35819335, n=21, 12-wk within-participant RCT) — longitudinal outcome data, not EMG — found overhead extension produced 19.9% triceps growth vs 13.5% for the neutral/pushdown-equivalent position. A ~40% relative advantage, driven by the long head being held lengthened in the overhead arm position (it crosses the shoulder).
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 3, with an unusually large effect size for a single-joint accessory.
**Sacrifices:** elbow-stress concerns relative to pushdowns exist only as blog-level anecdote, no RCT injury-incidence data. At 5 direct sets/wk (1-2 + 3-3) plus indirect load from 2 press movements, absolute joint loading stays modest.

### 2-2a / 3-4 — Standing DB curl (biceps primary, flipped 2026-07-31), free choice standing or incline
**Target:** biceps brachii; brachialis secondary.
**Why standing, not incline, as primary:** the original "shoulder extended = longer biceps = automatic stretch-mediated win" framing is mechanically imprecise for a free-weight implementation (see Honest uncertainty). A better-controlled 2024 study matching resistance profile via cable (PMC11906226, n=15 men, 10-wk, preacher vs Bayesian/cross-body cable curl) found **no significant length-position effect on hypertrophy when tension is held constant through the cable** — once the DB torque-curve confound is removed, "longer length wins" is unconfirmed. With no growth advantage either way, incline stops earning primary status on stimulus and standing wins on friction: it drops a shared incline-bench dependency with the incline-press slot (then A2a), a finite resource used twice in one session for no proven benefit. Not the same move as switching to a cable "true stretch" curl (refuted below) — that trades bench contention for cable-stack contention for the same zero benefit; standing trades it for nothing.
**Wins on:** criterion 2 directly, at equal expected growth (criteria 1, 3, 4) per the cited RCT.
**Sacrifices:** the loaded-stretch claim as originally stated overstated what the free-weight torque curve delivers (see Honest uncertainty) — no further loss from demoting incline to a fixed sub, since that claim was already unconfirmed.

### 3-1 — Romanian deadlift (hip hinge)
**Target:** hamstrings and glutes at long length; erectors secondary/isometric.
**Why RDL over back extension/hip thrust as primary:** loads the biarticular hamstrings and glutes at long muscle length under real load. The strongest published case for a *dedicated* hip-thrust slot (Barbalho 2020, ~10% greater glute hypertrophy) was retracted for improbable data. The better-controlled replacement (Vigotsky/Nunes 2023, PMC10593473) found squat/hinge and hip thrust produce *similar* gluteal hypertrophy, with squat/hinge additionally producing greater thigh hypertrophy. A hip-thrust slot buys nothing RDL + BSS don't already deliver, and would cost one of 13 fixed slots.
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 3.
**Sacrifices:** highest axial spinal loading in the program — a sequencing consideration, not a selection error. It never stacks with the quad-primary slot's heavy loading (old Template B only; now Day 3 alone). It shares Day 3 with BSS, the same pairing old Template B ran — worth watching during the load-ramp weeks given the program's tendon-lag caution, not severe enough to reorder or split.

### 2-1b — Lat pulldown or weighted pull-up (vertical pull)
**Target:** lats primary; biceps/rear delts secondary.
**Why an equal-value pair, with a measurability edge to pulldown:** no longitudinal hypertrophy RCT compares pulldown vs pull-up (only EMG/opinion pieces) — a genuine evidence gap, resolved by mechanism. Pulldown offers finer, more consistent load increments; weighted pull-up adds coarse jumps on top of full bodyweight, and during the return-to-training ramp there may be no added-weight increment available at all, stalling double progression on reps alone for weeks.
**Wins on:** criterion 2, marginally — not enough to force pulldown to sole-primary status, so both remain fungible.
**Sacrifices:** nothing growth-relevant; a logistics/measurability distinction.
**Lat long-length slot:** deliberately absent. A full-ROM pulldown/pull-up already reaches arms overhead — the lengthened lat position — so the loaded-stretch box is checked by the existing lift. No RCT found showing a straight-arm pullover adds hypertrophy beyond a full-ROM vertical pull plus a row, and a 14th exercise would violate the program's time-per-marginal-set logic.

### 2-1a — Flat DB press or dip (secondary horizontal push)
**Target:** mid/lower (sternal/costal) pec, triceps, anterior delt (dip more than flat press).
**Why this pairing, as a fixed swap rather than a coverage necessity:** Chaves 2020 already shows incline-only preserves mid/lower pec growth, so this slot isn't required for coverage — it adds chest/triceps/delt volume and angle variation on top of an already-safe primary (1-1a). Dip's higher anterior-delt/lower-pec demand is a bonus; no evidence found that dip carries higher injury incidence than flat press when load-matched at 1–2 RIR.
**Wins on:** criterion 1 (volume without a new exercise slot, via the fixed-swap mechanism).
**Sacrifices:** nothing — this slot's job was never to be the primary chest stimulus.

### 3-2 — Bulgarian split squat or hack squat (unilateral quad/glute)
**Target:** quads, glutes; hamstrings (long length in front-leg hip flexion) and adductors (hip-extension synergist) secondary.
**Why BSS over walking lunge/leg press as the unilateral slot:** deep unilateral hip flexion loads glutes/hamstrings/adductors at long muscle length — the same stretch-mediated mechanism the program elevates elsewhere — at lower knee-joint demand than a loaded free-standing squat, plus single-leg stabilizer coverage bilateral machines structurally cannot provide.
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 3, plus unique unilateral coverage.
**Sacrifices — audited and kept as-is:** DB-loaded BSS plausibly hits a grip/balance ceiling before the target muscle reaches 1–2 RIR once load climbs (vendor/coaching sources note straps become necessary), which could corrupt the criterion-2 progression signal. Not severe enough to change the pick. **Reopen condition:** if DB load approaches ~40–50 lb/hand and reps stall while legs still feel short of failure, add straps rather than swapping exercises.

### 1-3a / 4-3 — Chest-supported DB reverse fly (flipped to DB 2026-08-12; was reverse pec deck, itself flipped 2026-07-31)
**Target:** posterior deltoid.
**Why a dedicated isolation slot, not redundant with 1-1b/2-1b:** rows cued elbows-tucked-to-torso (as chest-supported row typically is) bias lats/mid-back over posterior delt, which needs horizontal shoulder abduction with elbows flared to be loaded directly. No overlap-quantifying RCT was found, but the biomechanical distinction (row's pulling vector vs fly's abduction vector) is uncontested, and the slot is cheap.
**Why the machine was picked in July, and why that reasoning did not survive contact:** the July argument was that the DB version needs a sustained hip-hinge hold, technique-sensitive (trap/lat compensation creeps in as the set fatigues), for no offsetting benefit — machine-vs-free-weight resistance curve being this framework's weakest criterion. That argument was correct about the *standing bent-over* DB fly and silently assumed it was the only DB version. It is not. **Chest-down on a 30–45° incline bench removes the hinge hold entirely** — the bench holds the torso angle, so the one named sacrifice disappears while the abduction vector is unchanged. The machine's remaining edge is cleaner pin increments, which is worth less than 2.5 lb dumbbells cost, and worth nothing at all when the station is occupied.
**Why it flipped:** 2026-08-12, Upper A — the pec deck was busy and all 3 sets were skipped. Zero rear delt work in the session. The user's own read: "nice but very busy." A station whose availability is a coin flip cannot hold the slot for the program's *least* well-dosed muscle. The DB version also collapses the 1-3a/1-3b superset onto a single station (DB rack), which removes the two-station-holding etiquette problem that the busy-gym rule otherwise has to arbitrate.
**Wins on:** criterion 3 (friction/availability) decisively; criteria 1 and 2 unchanged — same vector, same rep range, same progression line, and 2.5 lb DB steps are *finer* than the pin stack's 10 lb.
**Sacrifices:** fixed-path guidance, which the bench substantially replaces. Reverse pec deck stays as the named substitute — use it on the days it happens to be free, log it on the same line. Volume caveat unchanged: rear delts land at only ~5 direct sets/wk (1-3a + 4-3) plus fractional row credit — below the 10–14 target. Still flagged, still not fixed here; the flip makes those 5 sets *reliable*, which was the actual failure.
**Reopen condition:** if the DB version's reps stall while the target still feels short of failure, or trap/upper-back compensation shows up despite the bench, go back to the machine and accept the availability risk.

### 2-3 / 4-4 — Standing calf raise (full stretch, myo-reps both sessions)
**Target:** gastrocnemius primary, soleus secondary.
**Why standing over seated as primary:** Kinoshita et al. 2023 (Frontiers in Physiology, PMC10753835) found standing produced far greater gastrocnemius growth than seated (lateral gastroc 12.4% vs 1.7%, medial 9.2% vs 0.6%, d = 0.88–1.58), with similar soleus growth. The fixed-substitute note already flags seated as soleus-biased.
**Wins on:** criteria 1, 3.
**Sacrifices:** none in exercise choice — the sacrifice was in *volume* (finding 2, fixed by a second weekly slot, then A5, now 2-3). Myo-reps in both weekly sessions: same total volume, more engaging structure, one progression line (top-set load).

## Adversarial findings

### Confirmed — applied to program.md

1. **Rectus femoris is left almost untrained by design, and this was undocumented.** Every quad exercise (hack squat, leg press, BSS) is a simultaneous hip+knee-extension pattern, during which RF must minimize force output to avoid opposing hip extension (Ema et al. 2016). A 12-wk within-subject MRI RCT comparing leg press to knee extension (Kinoshita/Maeo 2026, MSSE) found RF grew +13.2% (p≤.001) with knee extension vs +1.1% (p=.379, ns) with leg press, despite comparable whole-quadriceps growth. **Resolution:** documented, not fixed with a 14th exercise — RF is ~10% of quad cross-sectional volume, whole-quad growth is comparable across conditions per the same RCT, and the design principle is to add sets to existing compounds rather than new exercises. program.md gained a paragraph naming the tradeoff and offering leg extension as an optional future swap for one BSS slot.

2. **Calf volume sat at the program's own stated "floor for any growth," not its 10–14 target — an unexplained internal inconsistency.** Program.md cites Iversen 2021 (~4 sets/wk floor) and a 10–14 target for every other muscle, then programmed calves at 4.5 sets/wk with no stated exception. A calf-specific RCT (PMID 38684187, Int J Sports Med, 2024, 6-wk, untrained women) found 12 sets/wk significantly outgrew 6 for both gastrocnemius and soleus with no plateau at 12, killing the "calves saturate early" defense; daily walking is sub-threshold, non-progressive loading that doesn't substitute for near-failure sets. Calf raises are also the cheapest volume in the program to add (short ROM, low fatigue cost). **Resolution:** a second standing calf raise slot (then A5, 3×8–15, RIR 0–1), reusing one of the 13 exercises rather than adding a 14th. Calves went 4.5 → ~9 sets/wk, matching quads/hams; now 2-3/4-4, myo-reps. Confidence: medium — the supporting RCT is a single 6-wk trial in untrained women, and extrapolation to a trained male over longer duration isn't certain. The core argument (the program's own rule applied inconsistently, cheap fix available) stands independent of it.

3. **Arm volume was mislabeled in the program's own summary line — a documentation bug, not a design flaw.** Program.md stated "biceps ~7.5 direct, triceps ~7.5 direct... land ~10–12 effective," but its own tables give 3 sets × 1.5 template-frequency = 4.5 direct sets/wk each (then A4a and A4b, Template A only), and ~9 effective under the program's own ~0.5 fractional-synergist credit. Chest and back both compute correctly to 9 direct, so the bug is isolated to arms — the side-delt figure (7.5, correct for side delts) was evidently copy-pasted onto the arm rows. **Resolution:** corrected to 4.5 direct / ~9 effective for both, not patched upward — per the program's cited dose-response curves the gain from ~9 to ~10–11 sets is small relative to added time, and there's no basis to treat arms differently from quads/hams once the number is right. No sets added.

### Refuted — challenged, survived, no change

- **"BSS's DB-load ceiling (grip/balance) corrupts the progression signal for a returning lifter."** Real mechanism, not severe enough to change the pick — reopen condition instead of a preemptive swap (see 3-2).
- **"Incline DB curl's stretch-bias rationale is mechanically flawed, so swap to a cable-based true-stretch curl."** The torque-curve critique is correct (see Honest uncertainty), but the best RCT controlling for the confound (PMC11906226) found no significant hypertrophy advantage to the shoulder-extended cable position over preacher — nothing to capture by switching, and it would add contention to an already cable-dependent superset block. (Incline DB curl was later demoted to a fixed sub on separate friction grounds — see 2-2a/3-4.)
- **"Zero direct ab/core training despite a dedicated abs.md — add a weighted crunch or hanging leg raise inside the rest window of the RDL/quad-primary slots at zero net session cost."** Refuted three ways. (1) *The insertion point is actively harmful.* RDL and the quad-primary compound both depend on rectus abdominis for anti-flexion bracing and intra-abdominal pressure under the heaviest axial/shear load in their sessions; loading that stabilizer between those sets pre-fatigues it going into the program's highest-stakes lifts, and hanging leg raises add grip fatigue that bleeds into RDL grip. (2) *"Zero net time" is false in this gym.* It requires holding a third station (cable stack or pull-up bar) on top of the two the antagonist supersets already occupy; the busy-gym rule says that either doesn't happen or costs real time. (3) *The effect size doesn't clear the bar.* abs.md's own numbers give RA true CSA gain of 5.68–8.82%, which that doc calls trivial-to-small, and it already resolved abs as a sequencing problem (build now, reveal via a later time-boxed cut). The stretch-bias inclusions elsewhere are also small (d≈0.20) but *free*; this one is small-effect, non-free, **and** risks the main lift. Compounds already deliver isometric bracing load to the RA. **If ab work is ever added, it gets its own slot** and pays real time for it — never a heavy-compound rest window.
- **"Lateral raise's identical appearance in both weekly slots is wasteful redundancy."** Side delts land at ~7.5–8 sets/wk, below the 10–14 target, so this isn't excess volume needing a trim, and no RCT (as opposed to EMG-only claims) supports rotating lateral-raise angle for extra growth. (Implement was later flipped cable → DB on friction grounds; the duplication itself stands.)
- **"Compound-before-isolation order (e.g. incline press pre-fatigues triceps before the triceps-extension slot) blunts the isolation set."** Mechanistically plausible, unsupported by matched-volume exercise-order RCTs, which show no effect on weekly hypertrophy outcomes.

## Friction audit

All 13 exercises scored against the adherence criterion, filtered through the anti-rationalization test above. Confidence on every row is **low-to-medium** — mechanism and practitioner report, not outcome-trial evidence. Kompf/Rhodes/Lee 2025 (PMID 40041305, Delphi expert-consensus ratings of exercise technical complexity) is the only instrument-grade source behind the skill-demand column, and it rates novice barriers, not measured dropout.

### Deliberately NOT made — the hard things stay hard

- **3-1 (Romanian deadlift, then B1) — no swap, only a usage nudge.** Worst setup, contention, and skill score of any lift here, and rejected as a swap target anyway: the discomfort is the stretch-mediated stimulus, so it fails the committed-counterfactual test. The approved sub (45° back extension / DB RDL) trades away real absolute-load capacity — a growth cost bigger than the friction it removes. Usage nudge only: use the sub proactively when the platform is busy.
- **3-2 (Bulgarian split squat, then B3a) — no swap, no dread-tracking demotion.** Highest setup cost and skill demand in the program. A proposal to track skip-rate and pre-commit to hack squat/walking lunge on that basis was rejected: BSS's value *is* the deep unilateral hip-flexion stretch under load, the same mechanism that makes it uncomfortable. "Watch and reopen on dread" would let avoidance masquerade as friction — what the anti-rationalization test exists to catch. The documented reopen condition (grip/balance ceiling → straps at ~40–50 lb/hand) remains the only trigger.
- **1-2/3-3 (Overhead cable triceps ext, then A4a) — no swap.** Its cable-stack contention dropped from "shared with the lateral-raise slot" to "sole user" as a side effect of the lateral-raise swap. Its ~40% relative stimulus edge over pushdowns — the largest single-exercise effect size in the program — is too large to trade for residual, now-minor setup friction.
- **Busy-gym double-occupation edge case (primary and sub both taken) — no new rule.** Replacing "do the other superset half and come back" with a flat "skip it" was rejected: the existing rule is one deterministic line, the added-decision-load claim is low-confidence and unmeasured, and the replacement costs an exercise's volume outright versus getting it later in the session.
- **Frequency held at 3×/week — no increase. SUPERSEDED 2026-07-31, see restructure above.** At the time, no adherence evidence favored higher frequency for a free-living lifter. That ruling assumed session *count* and session *length* were independent levers — they aren't: 3→4 sessions/week is the mechanism that cuts each session ~40% at fixed weekly volume. A length fix, not a frequency preference. The user later stated he doesn't mind 3–4 gym visits/week and wants shorter, less repetitive sessions — new information the ruling didn't have.

## Honest uncertainty

- **Biceps long-length debate is genuinely unresolved, not settled by either side.** PMID 39809454 (2024, n=63 women, 8-wk) found real regional dissociation — incline grew proximal biceps more, preacher grew distal more — so shoulder position *does* matter regionally. PMC11906226 (2024, n=15 men, 10-wk), which controlled the DB-torque-curve confound by loading through a cable, found no significant *overall* hypertrophy advantage to the shoulder-extended position. Not strictly contradictory (one measures regional distribution, the other overall growth), but together they mean "shoulder-extended = automatically superior via stretch" is not supportable as stated, while "shoulder position may matter for *where* the biceps grows" stays open. Program.md's stretch-bias line no longer cites incline curl for this reason.
- **DB curl's actual loaded-stretch delivery is mechanically questionable and unverified for this program.** Gravity's torque about the elbow approaches zero exactly when the forearm hangs vertical — the bottom, longest-muscle-length part of the rep — the classic ascending-then-descending DB strength curve. Incline DB curl may therefore reach the long shoulder-extended *position* without delivering meaningful *load* there, defeating the point of a stretch bias. No study was found measuring loaded tension at end-range specifically for incline DB curl (as opposed to cable-loaded stretch positions), so this stays a plausible-but-unverified concern.
- **Chest-supported row vs free-standing row for regional back hypertrophy has no direct RCT.** The pick rests entirely on the already-validated instability-lowers-force-output principle (program.md line 21). If a study appears and contradicts the mechanism, revisit.
- **Pulldown vs pull-up hypertrophy equivalence has no direct longitudinal RCT** — only EMG/opinion pieces. The measurability argument for pulldown holds on its own terms, but the growth-equivalence assumption is inferred from the general exercise-choice-doesn't-matter-at-matched-volume evidence, not tested head-to-head.
- **Pendulum squat as a second fixed sub for 4-1** was flagged as a low-severity, cost-free improvement by the lower-body lane but not added to program.md — it doesn't change the primary pick and was outside the confirmed-changes list this synthesis was scoped to apply.
